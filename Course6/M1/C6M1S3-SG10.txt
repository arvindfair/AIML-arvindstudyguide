Why is a probabilistic classifier considered more robust in real-world data scenarios than a deterministic one?


It expresses uncertainty, allowing decisions to reflect confidence levels.-Correct. Probabilistic classification models uncertainty explicitly through posterior probabilities, giving confidence-based decisions.

Which statement best explains the conceptual role of Bayes’ theorem in probabilistic classification?

It reverses the conditional probability to express how evidence updates a prior belief about a class.--Correct. Bayes’ theorem updates the prior belief P(C) into a posterior belief P(C∣X) once evidence X is observed.

Naïve Bayes assumes that all features are conditionally independent given the class label. What pedagogical reason justifies this 'naïve' assumption?

It simplifies computation while still performing well in many practical cases.--Correct. The assumption simplifies joint probability computation without major loss in many domains.

If two classes have overlapping Gaussian distributions for a feature, what does the model’s posterior P(C∣x) primarily depend on?

On the relative likelihood values under each class’s distribution and the class priors.--Correct. The model weighs likelihoods P(x∣C) and priors P(C) to compute posteriors even when distributions overlap.

Why might Bernoulli Naïve Bayes underperform on text data compared with Multinomial Naïve Bayes?

It treats multiple occurrences of the same word as a single event, losing information about frequency.--Correct. Bernoulli NB uses binary features (presence/absence), ignoring how often words appear, unlike Multinomial NB, which captures counts.
