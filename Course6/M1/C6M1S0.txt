Welcome to the course titled ‘Advanced Machine Learning’.

 

Machine learning continues to evolve rapidly, with new techniques and applications constantly expanding what intelligent systems can achieve. This course builds on core machine learning foundations and introduces advanced models, selection strategies, interpretability methods, and practical paradigms used in high-impact domains today.

 

You will begin with powerful supervised learning techniques such as Support Vector Machines and Naive Bayes, examining both their theoretical underpinnings and applied workflows. Throughout the course, we will focus on balancing mathematical understanding with hands-on implementation and evaluation.

Welcome to the module ‘Support Vector Machines and Naive Bayes’.  

 

This module introduces two classification models that represent contrasting approaches within machine learning. We will talk about Support Vector Machines (SVMs) and Naive Bayes models.  

 

SVMs take a geometric approach, identifying boundaries that best separate categories of data, while Naive Bayes relies on probability and independence assumptions to make fast, effective predictions. 

 

SVM introduces the powerful idea of hyperplanes as separators in multidimensional spaces, aiming not just to divide classes, but to do so with optimal confidence. As data becomes more complex, SVM extends naturally through soft margins and kernel methods, enabling flexible modelling of nonlinear relationships. Although SVMs can be applied to regression tasks as well, we will focus on their classification abilities only in this module. 

 

Naive Bayes, on the other hand, keeps computation light and assumptions simple. Built on Bayes’ Theorem, it applies conditional probability to classification problems, proving especially effective in domains such as text analytics where feature independence can be a reasonable approximation. Despite its simplicity, it remains a robust baseline model thanks to its efficiency, versatility, and strong performance on high-dimensional features. 

 

 

Learning Objectives 

 

By the end of this module, you should be able to: 

Explain the core principles behind SVMs and Naive Bayes as classification models 

Understand hyperplanes, margins, and the role of slack variables in SVM optimisation 

Recognise how kernel methods enable SVMs to model nonlinear decision boundaries 

Derive and interpret the Naive Bayes classifier starting from Bayes’ Theorem 

Distinguish between Gaussian, Multinomial, and Bernoulli Naive Bayes models and their appropriate use cases
